# Signage — the two printed QR pieces

Generated by `scripts/make-signage.py`. Edit the script, never the files; the
QR code is drawn from the same string that is printed under it, and a design
tool nudging a single module destroys the code invisibly.

```
python3 scripts/make-signage.py
```

| File | Object | Trim | Where the scan lands |
|---|---|---|---|
| `crease-counter-card-4x6.*` | Card in an acrylic counter holder | 4 × 6 in | `creasenyc.com/r` → `/order` |
| `crease-window-cling-5x5.*` | Die-cut decal, **outside** of the glass | 5 × 5 in | `creasenyc.com/w` → home page |
| `crease-window-cling-5x5-reverse.*` | Same decal, **inside** of the glass | 5 × 5 in | `creasenyc.com/w` → home page |

`.pdf` is what goes to the printer. `.svg` is the master. `.png` is a 300 dpi
proof for looking at, not for printing.

## Which window file

This is the only way to get this wrong, and it is not recoverable once the
decals arrive.

- **Applied to the outside of the glass** (adhesive on the back, ink facing
  the street) → `crease-window-cling-5x5.pdf`.
- **Applied to the inside of the glass** (adhesive on the front, pressed
  against the pane, read through the glass) → `crease-window-cling-5x5-reverse.pdf`.

Inside application is what most shop owners will do — it cannot be picked at
from the pavement and the weather never touches it — and it mirrors everything
seen from the street. A mirrored QR code does not scan: the three corner
squares are the scanner's coordinate system, and reversing them reverses the
reading order of every bit in the code. No phone setting undoes it. Printers
call this "second surface" or "reverse print"; if the order form asks, that is
the question it is asking.

Both files look identical at thumbnail size and nothing a print shop checks
will tell them apart. Say which one in the order, in words.

## What to ask the printer for

**Counter card** — 4 × 6 in, 16pt matte or silk card stock, full colour one
side, no lamination needed. Artwork is supplied at 4.25 × 6.25 in: that is the
trim size plus 0.125 in bleed on every edge, which is what the upload form
expects. Drop it in a $3 slant-back acrylic sign holder — 4 × 6 is the size
every stock holder takes, so a reprint costs a few dollars and needs no new
hardware.

**Window decal** — 5 × 5 in, die cut to the rounded square, outdoor-rated
vinyl, 3–5 year. Two things to specify:

1. **White ink underbase across the whole decal.** On clear vinyl without it,
   the white of the artwork is not printed — it is left transparent — and the
   QR code ends up floating over whatever is inside the shop. No phone will
   read it. Ordering plain white vinyl instead of clear-with-underbase gets
   the same result and costs less; the die cut is what makes it look
   deliberate on glass, not the transparency.
2. **Removable adhesive**, or a static cling if the shop is nervous. A shop
   owner who thinks it is permanent says no at the door.

## Why the artwork is the way it is

**Error correction is level H on both.** These URLs are short enough that the
strongest level still fits in the same 29-module code that the weakest would
use — so 30% redundancy is free. Thirty percent of the code can be lost to
glare, a thumbprint, a sun-faded corner or a strip of tape and it still reads.

**The modules are pure black, not brand green.** Green on white clears the
contrast a scanner needs on paper. The window piece is read through glass with
the sky reflected in it, by a phone at arm's length, and reflection eats
contrast. The green is everywhere else on the piece.

**The quiet zone is asserted, not eyeballed.** `scripts/make-signage.py` fails
rather than writes if the clear space around a code drops under four modules.
A short quiet zone is the defect that survives every human review: it looks
perfect, it decodes on the designer's phone an inch away in good light, and it
fails against a dark shop interior.

**The printed URL is a short stable path, not the destination.** `/r` and `/w`
resolve in `apps/web/lib/scan.ts` at request time. That is what lets the App
Store listing, when it exists, take over the scan on an iPhone without
reprinting anything already stuck to a window — and it is how the two
placements are told apart afterwards. nginx logs the path, so the traffic count
in `growth/traffic.py` separates counter cards from window clings with no tag,
no cookie, and nothing for an ad-blocker to strip.

## Before ordering a hundred of anything

Print one of each on a home printer, tape the window one to actual glass, and
scan both from where a real person would stand — a customer's arm's length at
the counter, the pavement outside the window. A code that reads on a screen is
not evidence that it reads on a window.
